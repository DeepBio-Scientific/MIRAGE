\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Co-folding models such as Boltz-2 and Nesso-1 report protein--ligand affinity correlations of
Pearson $\approx 0.6$ and are proposed as fast surrogates for free-energy perturbation. We show
that this headline accuracy is largely \emph{interpolation within protein families already
represented in public structural data}, not prediction for novel targets. The central result is
an interaction: frontier co-folders depend strongly on protein-family support while
family-disjoint shallow controls do not. Defining the \emph{family generalization gap}
$G_m = r_m(S_f{\ge}301) - r_m(S_f{=}1)$ over matched-affinity strata, we measure
$G_{\text{Nesso}}=0.45$ (95\% CI $[0.27,0.61]$) and $G_{\text{Boltz}}=0.62$ ($[0.12,1.01]$),
versus $G_{\text{RF}}=-0.01$ and $G_{\text{kNN}}=-0.03$ (CIs spanning zero) for a random-forest
QSAR model and a ligand-$k$NN baseline under family-disjoint cross-validation. Family support
predicts accuracy after controlling for ligand similarity, protein length, publication date,
family affinity variance, measurement type, and affinity range (Nesso $t=-8.1$; Boltz $t=-3.7$);
a two-dimensional analysis localizes the dependence to the \emph{protein-family} axis, not ligand
chemistry. On novel families the practical ranking inverts: a shallow family-disjoint random
forest ($r=0.41$) beats both co-folders ($0.32$, $0.31$). A prospective, structurally novel
post-cutoff target (OpenBind EV-A71 2A protease) supplies external confirmation: no co-folder
reliably exceeds a molecular-weight baseline. Applying the same ligand-only control to the standard
LBA30 benchmark---which no prior work reports---molecular weight alone ($r=0.44$) matches or beats
most of the published leaderboard. We call this effect \emph{redundancy-driven
inflation} / \emph{training-familiarity dependence} rather than leakage, and release FamBench---a
plug-in benchmark and dataset that measures accuracy across the full family-support curve, with
family-disjoint controls and two-level bootstrap inference.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Predicting protein--ligand binding affinity from structure is central to computational drug
discovery. Co-folding models---AlphaFold3 \citep{abramson2024alphafold3}, Boltz-1/2
\citep{wohlwend2024boltz1,passaro2025boltz2}, Chai-1 \citep{chai2024chai1}, Nesso-1
\citep{valencelabs2026nesso1}, TerraBind \citep{terray2026terrabind}---increasingly predict
affinity directly, with Boltz-2 approaching free-energy-perturbation accuracy
\citep{passaro2025boltz2}. These models are summarized by a single correlation on a held-out set.
That number cannot separate a model that has learned binding physics from one that recognizes
protein families it has seen many times in the PDB. The distinction is decisive: a medicinal
chemistry program succeeds or fails on \emph{novel} targets---those least represented in public
structural and affinity data.

Prior work shows ligand-based and docking benchmarks reward memorization
\citep{wallach2018memorization,chen2019dude}, that affinity networks rely on train--test
similarity rather than interaction physics \citep{volkov2022frustration,kanakala2023latentbiases},
and that re-splitting lowers apparent accuracy \citep{li2023leakproof}. What is missing is a
quantitative, model-agnostic account of how co-folding affinity accuracy depends on
\emph{protein-family support}, and a benchmark that treats that support as the independent
variable.

\paragraph{Central claim.} Standard affinity benchmarks largely measure interpolation within
protein families already represented in public structural and affinity data. FamBench measures
transfer to unseen protein families. Under matched affinity distributions and family-disjoint
controls, Nesso-1 and Boltz-2 improve sharply with family redundancy but collapse on singleton
families, where a shallow random forest performs better. A prospective OpenBind target confirms
that current co-folders may fail to reliably exceed simple physicochemical baselines.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
  \item \textbf{A model-class $\times$ family-support interaction.} We define the family
  generalization gap $G_m$ and show it is large and significant for co-folders and
  indistinguishable from zero for family-disjoint controls (Sec.~\ref{sec:gap}).
  \item \textbf{Familiarity dependence, controlled.} Pre-cutoff family support predicts accuracy
  after ligand similarity, protein length, date, family affinity variance, measurement type, and
  affinity range; the dependence localizes to the protein-family axis, not ligand chemistry
  (Sec.~\ref{sec:controls}). A family-mean predictor recovers most of the ``accuracy'' from family
  identity alone.
  \item \textbf{Practical and prospective consequences.} On novel families the ranking inverts;
  on a novel post-cutoff target neither co-folder beats molecular weight (Secs.~\ref{sec:novel},
  \ref{sec:temporal}).
  \item \textbf{FamBench}, a plug-in benchmark and dataset with a two-dimensional
  (family-support $\times$ ligand-similarity) design, family-disjoint controls, and a two-level
  bootstrap reporting protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:design}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Co-folding affinity models.} AlphaFold2 \citep{jumper2021alphafold2} was extended to
protein--ligand complexes by AlphaFold3 \citep{abramson2024alphafold3} and open reimplementations
Boltz-1 \citep{wohlwend2024boltz1}, Chai-1 \citep{chai2024chai1}, RoseTTAFold-All-Atom
\citep{krishna2024rfaa}. Boltz-2 \citep{passaro2025boltz2}, Nesso-1 \citep{valencelabs2026nesso1},
and TerraBind \citep{terray2026terrabind} predict affinity directly, evaluated by overall
correlation.

\paragraph{Scoring and QSAR baselines.} Empirical (Vina \citep{trott2010vina}, smina
\citep{koes2013smina}) and learned (RF-Score \citep{ballester2010rfscore}, Gnina
\citep{mcnutt2021gnina}, AEV-PLIG \citep{valsson2025aevplig}) functions are trained and tested on
PDBbind \citep{liu2017pdbbind} / CASF-2016 \citep{su2019casf}, with labels from BindingDB
\citep{gilson2016bindingdb} and ChEMBL \citep{mendez2019chembl}, against FEP+
\citep{wang2015fep}.

\paragraph{Memorization and benchmark critiques.} Wallach and Heifets
\citep{wallach2018memorization} and Chen et al.\ \citep{chen2019dude} traced ligand-based/docking
``success'' to dataset bias; Volkov et al.\ \citep{volkov2022frustration} found ligand-only and
protein-only models match full-complex models and nearest-neighbor baselines are already strong.
Kanakala et al.\ \citep{kanakala2023latentbiases} documented latent biases; MISATO
\citep{siebenmorgen2024misato} re-curated data.

\paragraph{Positioning against recent split-aware benchmarks.} FamBench differs from three recent
efforts by making \emph{protein-family support} the primary axis on \emph{frozen frontier
co-folders}. Runs N' Poses \citep{runsnposes2025} studies pose recovery versus training
similarity; FamBench studies affinity versus family support. NTAB \citep{ntab2025} stratifies
post-cutoff ChEMBL assays by \emph{ligand} similarity; FamBench makes protein-family familiarity
the main axis while controlling ligand novelty. Leak-Proof PDBBind \citep{li2023leakproof}
constructs protected splits and \emph{retrains} conventional scoring functions; FamBench audits
frozen frontier co-folders across the full family-support curve and adds prospective evaluation.
Nesso-1 \citep{valencelabs2026nesso1} reports chemical-similarity analyses but explicitly lacks a
disclosed target sequence/structure-similarity analysis; FamBench fills that missing evaluation.
Sheridan \citep{sheridan2013timesplit} motivates the time-split view our OpenBind arm adopts.

\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:gap}

\paragraph{Family support.} For a target in family $f$ we define the pre-cutoff support
$S_f$ as the number of PDBbind structures in the same MMseqs2 \citep{steinegger2017mmseqs2} 30\%
sequence-identity cluster. Because our evaluation complexes are all deposited before the models'
2021-09-30 training cutoff, $S_f$ is a count of structures the models could have seen for that
family. We bin $S_f$ into $\{1, 2\text{--}5, 6\text{--}20, 21\text{--}80, 81\text{--}300, 301+\}$.

\paragraph{Matched strata.} Within each family-support bin we compute Pearson $r$ between
predicted and measured affinity over a \emph{matched} $\mathrm{p}K$ window $[4.5, 8.0]$, holding
the label spread (and thus attainable correlation) approximately constant across bins.

\paragraph{Family generalization gap.}
\begin{equation}
G_m \;=\; r_m(S_f \ge 301) \;-\; r_m(S_f = 1),
\end{equation}
the change in accuracy from singleton to well-supported families. A positive $G_m$ means accuracy
is bought by family support. We estimate $G_m$ with a \emph{two-level bootstrap}: resample
families with replacement, then resample ligands within each family, so the confidence interval
reflects both family-level and ligand-level variability.

\paragraph{Controls.} The honest counterpart to a frozen co-folder---whose test families are in
its training window---is a model that \emph{cannot} have seen the test family. We evaluate a
random-forest QSAR model (RF-QSAR) and a ligand-$k$NN baseline under 5-fold \emph{family-disjoint}
cross-validation (\texttt{GroupKFold} on family id), so every prediction is on a held-out family.

\section{The FamBench Benchmark}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Redundancy set.} $18{,}759$ PDBbind-derived complexes with an exact $K_d/K_i/$IC$_{50}$
label ($\mathrm{p}K$), each annotated with protein-family support $S_f$ and ligand
nearest-neighbor ECFP4 Tanimoto. A balanced $3{,}360$-target ``core'' subset (560 per support bin)
is provided for quick evaluation.

\paragraph{Temporal set.} $649$ compounds with measured $K_d$ against one structurally novel,
post-2021 target---the OpenBind EV-A71 2A protease \citep{openbind2026dataset} (CC0)---with
molecular-weight, clogp, Gnina, smina, AEV-PLIG, and AQ-Affinity baselines shipped.

\paragraph{Two-dimensional design.} FamBench stratifies by \emph{protein-family support
$\times$ ligand similarity}, separating two memorization channels: co-folders may exploit
protein-family familiarity while ligand models may exploit chemical similarity. Matching affinity
distributions alone does not isolate these channels.

\paragraph{Reporting protocol.} We recommend: equal-family weighting (not per-complex);
mean within-stratum correlation rather than pooled-across-target correlation; Pearson, Spearman,
centered RMSE, and pairwise ranking accuracy; a two-level (family then ligand) bootstrap;
sensitivity to the clustering choice; and a baseline suite---family-mean (target identity),
molecular weight, clogp, ligand-$k$NN, and RF-QSAR. The prospective OpenBind score is reported as
\emph{one external target}, not population-level proof.

\section{Experimental Setup}

We evaluate models in four classes. \textbf{Affinity co-folders:} Boltz-2
\citep{passaro2025boltz2} (cuEquivariance kernels, ColabFold MSAs) and Nesso-1
\citep{valencelabs2026nesso1} (ESM-2 embeddings \citep{rives2021esm}), from public weights at
default settings; both output $\log_{10}(\text{IC}_{50})$, negated to a higher-is-stronger score.
\textbf{Confidence-proxy co-folders:} Chai-1 \citep{chai2024chai1} and ESMFold2, which predict
structure and an interface confidence (ipTM) but \emph{not} affinity; we score their ipTM as a
proxy, clearly labeled. \textbf{Family-disjoint controls:} RF-QSAR (ECFP4 $+$ amino-acid
composition) and ligand-$k$NN, both under family-disjoint CV. \textbf{Trivial/identity baselines:}
family-mean, molecular weight, clogp. AlphaFold3 has open code but request-only weights and no
affinity head; it is provided as a pluggable stub, not evaluated.

\subsection{Familiarity dependence, isolated by controls}
\label{sec:controls}

\paragraph{It survives every covariate.} Regressing per-target absolute error on
$\log_{10} S_f$ together with ligand similarity, protein length, publication year, family affinity
variance, measurement type, and the affinity regime ($\mathrm{p}K,\mathrm{p}K^2$), the family-
support coefficient remains large and significant: Nesso $-0.155$ ($t=-8.1$), Boltz $-0.157$
($t=-3.7$). The dependence is not explained by any measured confound.

\paragraph{The channel is protein-family, not ligand chemistry.} Table~\ref{tab:2d} stratifies
Nesso-1 accuracy by family support $\times$ ligand similarity. Accuracy climbs down the family
axis ($0.1 \to 0.5$) but is flat across the ligand axis. The memorization channel for co-folders
is protein-family familiarity.

\subsection{On novel families the ranking inverts}
\label{sec:novel}

The practical consequence is a rank inversion. On novel families ($S_f \le 5$), the family-disjoint
random forest ($r=0.411$) \emph{outperforms} both co-folders (Nesso $0.324$, Boltz $0.308$), and
ligand-$k$NN ($0.350$) is competitive. A $10^3$-parameter model with no structure beats
billion-parameter co-folders on exactly the regime that matters for a new target.

\subsection{Prospective confirmation on a novel target}
\label{sec:temporal}

\subsection{Confidence-proxy co-folders}
\label{sec:proxy}

Chai-1 and ESMFold2 predict structure and an interface confidence (ipTM), not affinity. Scoring
their ipTM as an affinity proxy (Table~\ref{tab:proxy}), the signal is weaker than the
affinity-head models, and the two proxies differ instructively. Chai-1 (overall Pearson $0.39$,
$n{=}598$) \emph{itself} carries the family-support dependence---rising from $0.16$ on novel
families to $0.46$ on redundant ones (family-support slope $t=-3.8$)---so the familiarity
dependence is a property of the learned structural representation, not only of a trained affinity
head. ESMFold2, by contrast (overall $0.18$, complete $n{=}599$ via the hosted API), is a weak and noisy
affinity proxy with \emph{no} significant family-support dependence ($t=-0.6$); this is consistent
with ESMFold2 being optimized for protein--protein interfaces rather than small molecules, so its
protein--ligand ipTM is poorly calibrated for affinity. AlphaFold3 would enter this category but
its weights are request-only.

\subsection{The missing control on a standard benchmark: LBA30}
\label{sec:lba30}

Our thesis is not specific to the FamBench dataset. ATOM3D LBA \citep{townshend2021atom3d} is the
field's standard structure-based affinity-generalization benchmark: PDBbind-refined complexes split
by 30\% protein sequence identity (``LBA30''; 3507 train / 490 test), on which recent geometric
deep nets report large gains---IPBind \citep{li2025ipbind} reports Pearson $0.732$, a claimed 19.6\%
relative improvement. Yet no published LBA30 result reports a ligand-only, molecular-weight, or QSAR
control; the field compares only against other structure/sequence deep nets. We supply that control
(Table~\ref{tab:lba30}). A ligand-only random forest (ECFP4, no protein) scores $0.457$ and
molecular weight \emph{alone} scores $0.438$.

Two conclusions follow, and we state both fairly. First, the top methods genuinely learn structural
signal: IPBind's $0.732$ clears the ligand-only ceiling by $\approx 0.28$, so it is not ligand
memorization. Second, and more consequential for the field, molecular weight alone matches or
exceeds much of the published LBA30 leaderboard---above ENN ($0.389$), on par with DeepDTA
($0.472$), and within $0.10$ of 3DCNN ($0.550$) and GNN ($0.545$). Small reported margins over these
methods were never contextualized against a trivial ligand descriptor. The same control also
reconciles LBA30 with FamBench: the 30\%-\emph{sequence} split is a far weaker generalization test
than singleton-family stratification---ligand-only reaches $0.46$ on LBA30 but collapses to
$0.31$--$0.41$ on FamBench novel families---so $0.73$ (LBA30) and $0.31$ (FamBench novel) do not
contradict; they measure different stringencies.

A related benchmark, CORDIAL \citep{brown2025cordial}, is explicitly designed to exclude fold/family
identity and validated leave-superfamily-out; it too reports no ligand-only or trivial baseline. The
absence of such controls across the generalization-benchmark literature is precisely the gap
FamBench institutionalizes.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Why not ``leakage.''} Our evidence establishes \emph{training-familiarity dependence}
and \emph{redundancy-driven inflation}: accuracy scales with pre-cutoff family support after
controlling for confounds, and matched family-disjoint models do not share the dependence. We
deliberately avoid the word ``leakage,'' which should be reserved for evaluations where related
families demonstrably occur across a claimed train/test boundary. We cannot reconstruct the exact
proprietary affinity training sets of the co-folders, so we make the weaker, defensible claim:
public affinity benchmarks are dominated by families the models are familiar with, and that
familiarity---not binding physics---carries most of the reported correlation.

\paragraph{Consequences.} Co-folders are the most accurate methods on well-supported families;
the problem is the standard practice of reporting one overall correlation, which conflates
interpolation within familiar families with transfer to novel ones. The honest number for a new
target is the novel-family value ($\approx 0.1$--$0.3$), and a cheap family-disjoint QSAR is a
strong, rarely-reported prospective baseline.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The redundancy set is pre-cutoff PDBbind; $S_f$ is a within-corpus
support proxy, not the models' exact training counts. The temporal set is a single target with a
congeneric series. We evaluate frozen public weights and do not retrain on de-leaked data. Family
support at 30\% identity is one axis; Pfam- and pocket-based clustering are natural sensitivity
analyses. The confidence-proxy results use ipTM, not a trained affinity head.

\section{Reproducibility and Artifact}

FamBench is an installable package (\texttt{pip install fambench}) with a Hugging Face dataset.
Scoring a model requires wrapping it as \texttt{predict(sequence, smiles)$\to$float} and calling
\texttt{run\_redundancy} / \texttt{run\_temporal}; a CLI scores a predictions CSV. All metrics,
controls ($G_m$, matched-$\mathrm{p}K$, multivariate regression, two-level bootstrap), baselines,
and the dataset pipeline are included; every number here is reproduced by the shipped harness from
released predictions.

\section{Conclusion}

Standard affinity benchmarks largely measure interpolation within familiar protein families.
FamBench re-centers evaluation on transfer to unseen families: the family generalization gap
separates frontier co-folders (large, positive) from family-disjoint controls (zero), a
prospective novel target confirms neither co-folder reliably beats molecular weight, and the fix
is to report the gap and the novel-family number rather than a single correlation.
